Phase 1 · Discovery & Longlist

Find your top AI use cases, step by step.

Three prompts. Run them one after another in the same LLM chat. Wait for the trainer between each. By the end you'll have a scored, clustered, ranked list of AI use cases for your business.

How this works (tap to expand)

Step 1: Open your preferred LLM (ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini, free tier works). Keep this tab open throughout.

Step 2: When the trainer says "go," tap Prompt 1 to copy it, paste it into your LLM.

Step 3: Wait for trainer instructions before pasting Prompt 2 into the SAME chat.

Step 4: Same for Prompt 3, paste it into the same chat after the next trainer instruction.

Step 5: At the end, you'll have a scored, clustered, ranked list of AI use cases. Share your P1 anonymously in Mentimeter.

Important: Keep all 3 prompts in the SAME conversation. The AI builds on its previous answers.

Step 1 · Discovery (longlist)

When the trainer says "go," tap the prompt below to copy it.

This generates your initial longlist of 5 to 7 AI use cases. No scoring yet, just ideas.

I'm a startup founder. Help me identify AI use cases for my business. My business in 2 sentences: [describe your business, what you do, who you sell to] Brainstorm 5 to 7 specific AI use cases for my business by answering these 3 questions: 1. What recurring process steps or tasks happen in my day-to-day work? 2. In which daily activities do I work with many documents, or need to research / search for specific content? 3. Where do I have large amounts of unstructured data that are currently only partially used or processed manually? IMPORTANT: NUMBER every use case clearly (1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7). This number is the permanent ID of that use case, you will reuse it in later steps. For each numbered use case, give: - # (the number) - Name (1 short line) - Description (2 sentences max) - Which of the 3 questions above it answers Do NOT score or rank yet. Just give me the numbered longlist.

Wait here. The trainer will move to Step 2 in a moment.

Step 2 · Scoring

Paste this into the SAME chat as Step 1.

This scores every use case from your list. Impact = business value. Feasibility = how realistic it is to build now.

Good. Now score every use case from your numbered list with two dimensions: IMPACT score (1 to 5): How much business value would this create? - Consider efficiency gain, cost reduction, customer experience, new revenue - 5 = high impact, transforms the business - 1 = marginal, nice-to-have FEASIBILITY score (1 to 5): How realistic is it with today's tech and our current resources? - Consider available data, tools, infrastructure, team skills - 5 = easy to build this week with off-the-shelf tools - 1 = needs major investment or capabilities we don't have Present your output as a compact table with these columns: # | Use Case (short name only) | Impact | Feasibility | Combined Score (Impact + Feasibility) Use the SAME number from the longlist as the # column. Keep the use case name short, one line. Sort by Combined Score, highest first. Add one short sentence below the table explaining the top-scoring use case.

Phase 2 · Cluster Prompts

Pick your cluster. Pick a prompt. Run it.

Head to your cluster's corner of the room. Each cluster has 2 prompts, pick whichever fits your use case best and run it in your LLM.

Cluster 1

Knowledge & RAG

Internal search, knowledge assistants, document summarization.

Exercise 1 — Build your internal knowledge assistant blueprint

Define what an internal knowledge assistant looks like for your startup.

I run a startup. Here's what we do in 2 sentences: [Your business in 2 sentences] Our team currently struggles to find information across: [List 3 to 5 places: Slack, Notion, Google Drive, emails, Confluence, etc.] A question our team asks at least 3 times a week is: [A real recurring question] Act as an AI architect. Help me: 1. Define what an "internal knowledge assistant" would look like for us 2. Identify the top 3 documents/sources we'd need to feed it 3. Suggest the simplest tool we can start with this week (NotebookLM, ChatGPT Projects, or Claude Projects, and why) 4. Outline the 5 most likely questions it should answer perfectly first 5. Flag one risk or limitation we should know upfront

Exercise 2 — Find the 5 questions your team asks weekly

Surface the recurring questions an AI assistant should answer instantly.

I run a startup that does: [Your business in 2 sentences] If you imagine a typical day in our company, predict the 5 most likely questions our team members would ask repeatedly when working, questions that today require digging through documents, asking colleagues, or checking multiple sources. For each question: - State it in natural language (how a team member would ask it) - Identify what type of document or system would hold the answer (FAQ, pricing doc, product spec, sales playbook, customer history, etc.) - Rate the "annoyance level" (1 to 10) of having to look it up manually - Suggest a 2-sentence ideal AI answer End with: "Which question, if answered instantly by AI, would have the biggest impact on this startup's daily flow? Why?"
Cluster 2

Automation

Workflows, repetitive tasks, data flows.

Exercise 1 — Map your workflow and find automation points

Take a real recurring manual process and find what to automate first.

I run a startup. Here's a recurring manual process that costs us time: Process name: [e.g., "Onboarding a new customer", "Processing inbound leads", "Following up on demo requests", "Generating weekly investor updates"] How often it happens: [e.g., 5 times per week] The steps I currently do manually: 1. [Step] 2. [Step] 3. [Step] [List all manual steps you do] Act as an automation consultant. Help me: 1. Identify which steps can be fully automated, partially automated, or must stay human 2. Suggest one tool per automatable step (Make, n8n, Zapier, or an LLM call) 3. Estimate hours per week saved if I implemented this 4. Flag THE ONE step that would unlock the most value if automated first 5. Tell me what could go wrong and how to prevent it

Exercise 2 — Design your first Make/n8n workflow blueprint

Get a step-by-step plan for a real workflow you can build this week.

I want to build a simple AI-powered automation. Here's the goal: Trigger: [What starts the workflow? E.g., "A new entry in our Typeform", "A new email from a specific sender", "A scheduled time each day"] End result: [What should happen at the end? E.g., "Lead is added to CRM with AI-generated qualification score", "Daily summary of new GitHub issues sent to Slack", "Personalized welcome email is drafted and saved as a draft"] My tools available: Make / n8n / Zapier (any of them) Walk me through how to build this: 1. Which trigger node should I use? 2. What data transformations are needed in between? 3. Where exactly does an LLM call fit in, and which model? 4. What's the final output action? 5. What's likely to break, and how do I add error handling? 6. Bonus: How could I test this safely without spamming anyone? Keep it minimal. I can always extend later.
Cluster 3

Customer Communication

Chatbots, outbound, support, voice agents, sales emails.

Exercise 1 — Draft your AI-powered cold outreach

Three versions of a cold email, each with a different psychological hook.

I'm a founder at a startup. Here's the context: My business in one sentence: [e.g., "We help mid-sized SaaS companies reduce churn through predictive customer health scoring"] My ideal customer is: - Industry: [e.g., B2B SaaS, mid-market] - Role: [e.g., Head of Customer Success, COO, CRO] - Pain we solve: [the specific pain] Here's a real-sounding prospect I want to reach: Company: [Real company you've thought of, OR invent a plausible one] What they do: [1 sentence] Why I think they need us: [1 sentence] Write 3 versions of a cold outreach email: - Version A: Direct value proposition - Version B: Question-first approach - Version C: Pattern-interrupt or curiosity hook Each version max 80 words. Include subject lines. Write like a real founder talks, no corporate cringe, no buzzwords, no "I hope this finds you well".

Exercise 2 — Build your support response template generator

Categorize support messages and design templates for what to automate.

We get repetitive customer questions at our startup. Imagine 3 typical customer messages we likely receive (you can invent realistic examples based on this business): My business in one sentence: [Your business] For each invented customer message: 1. Categorize it into a recurring "support pattern" (e.g., billing question, feature request, technical issue, refund, onboarding confusion) 2. Draft a response template that's empathetic, solution-oriented, and 5 sentences max 3. Flag which categories are SAFE to fully automate (AI sends directly) vs. which need human review before sending 4. Suggest a simple workflow: "If category = X, AI drafts → human approves → sends" 5. Estimate how much support time per week this could save a startup like ours
Cluster 4

Analysis & Insights

Reports, forecasting, anomaly detection, feedback clustering.

Exercise 1 — Generate sample data + first AI-driven insight

No data with you? Let the AI generate a realistic dataset and analyze it.

I'm a startup founder. I don't have real data with me right now, but I want to learn how AI can help me find insights in business data. My business in one sentence: [Your business] Step 1: Generate a realistic sample dataset for my startup. Choose ONE of these scenarios that fits my business: - Sales pipeline (50 leads with status, source, deal size, days in stage) - User signups (50 entries with signup source, plan, activation status) - Customer feedback (30 NPS responses with score and free-text comment) - Churn data (40 customers with tenure, plan, churn reason) Step 2: Now analyze that data and tell me: - 5 things that stand out - 3 non-obvious patterns I might have missed - 2 hypotheses about WHY those patterns exist - 1 immediate action I can take based on this insight - What chart would best visualize the most important finding Treat me as a smart founder who isn't a data scientist.

Exercise 2 — Cluster unstructured feedback into actionable themes

Turn messy support tickets, NPS comments, and call notes into clear themes.

I'm a startup founder. I want to learn how to use AI to turn messy unstructured feedback into action. My business in one sentence: [Your business] Step 1: Invent 20 realistic pieces of unstructured customer feedback that a startup like mine would receive. Mix sources: - Support tickets - NPS comments - Sales call notes - Churn interview snippets - App store reviews Make them realistic in tone, some emotional, some factual, some vague, some specific. Step 2: Now analyze that feedback and: 1. Cluster the entries into 5 to 7 themes (you name them) 2. Count how many entries fall into each theme 3. Identify the 2 themes with highest emotional intensity (positive or negative) 4. For the top 3 themes, suggest one concrete product or process change 5. Flag any single entry that's worth a direct founder call to that customer End with: "If you had to act on only ONE theme this quarter, here's why it should be: [your reasoning]"

Your 7-day plan

So the workshop doesn't stay at "aha", it becomes "doing".

Day 1 to 2

Write your top use case as a 1-pager

Problem, target user, expected outcome, success criterion.

Day 3

Set up the tool stack

Pick the "start now" tools, create accounts, run a first test.

Day 4 to 5

Build a 60% quality prototype

Better rough and honestly tested than polished and unused.

Day 6

Test with 3 real users from your target group

Gather quick feedback. Note what surprised you.

Day 7

Join the cluster follow-up (May 28 to 30)

Show what works, plan next iteration with peers.

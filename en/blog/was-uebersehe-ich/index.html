You don't know what you don't know

A chance remark from a stranger, and suddenly a door opens that you didn't know existed. That's exactly what AI can do, if you ask it the right way.

Share: LinkedIn Email X Facebook

The story that shows it all

A while ago I was in a coaching session with a client, an executive. She wanted to know what AI actually does for her, concretely. Instead of a definition, I told her an old story.

Many years ago, when I was selling GoPro cameras, I got talking with a customer. In the middle of the sales conversation he mentioned, in passing, that students ride along on sailing boats. Just coming aboard for crossings they'd never have planned themselves.

A completely random conversation. And it opened a door for me that I didn't know existed. The possibility had been there the whole time. It just sat completely outside anything I'd ever have searched for.

That's exactly the point most people miss with AI.

Search engines reward the one who already knows

A search engine is a tool for people who already know what they're looking for. You type in what you already have words for. Whatever lies outside your horizon of experience, you never type, because it never even occurs to you.

And that's the real problem with deciding. You're not missing the answer. You're missing the question.

The strongest prompt doesn't fetch an answer, it shows your blind spot

Most people use AI like a faster search engine: "Give me X." That gets you a good answer to a question you already had anyway.

The leverage is somewhere else. Ask the machine about what you can't see:

  • "What risks am I missing here?"
  • "What questions should I ask my team before I decide?"
  • "What options am I not thinking of right now?"

Those are prompts that don't fetch an answer. They make your blind spot visible.

The strongest prompt isn't "give me X", it's "what am I missing about X".

In practice: the impulse from outside

Experienced decision-makers have a tunnel. They're excellent at what they know, and blind to what lies outside their pattern. Experience builds a strong pattern, and a strong pattern has strong edges.

That's exactly where good AI use comes in. Not as an autopilot that takes over the decision, but as the unexpected impulse from outside that puts a third option on the table. Like the customer back then with his sailing remark. The difference: with the customer, you had to wait. The machine you can ask any time.

A concrete everyday example: an AI agent reads the transcript of a twenty-minute video and reports back only what's new compared to what you already know. You no longer watch the whole video. You get exactly the part that widens your horizon.

What to take away

Anyone who uses AI only as a faster answer machine leaves its biggest lever on the table. The value isn't in the answer to your question. It's in the question you'd never have asked.

"You don't know what you don't know" isn't a weakness. It's exactly the point where the right question to the machine moves you forward.

Welcome

Be a Leader in AI, Part 2 · 17th School for Young Leaders · Ohrid · Wednesday, 4 November 2026 · Jakub Popluhar

Last week you learned how a boss briefs. Tonight you hire your assistant: one that knows your goals and looks out for them.

What you leave with

  1. A named assistant that knows your goals.
  2. A one-page plan for one real goal, with a first step this week and a date.
  3. A Monday ritual that keeps it alive.

The first two are the minimum. Everything after that is a bonus.

How this works

  • Tap any grey box and it is copied. Then paste it into your AI.
  • Find your own spot in the room. Talk to your AI, don't type. Dictating is three times faster.
  • Back in your seat at 18:52. Stuck? Raise your hand, I come to you.
  • Private stays private. If something is personal, type it instead of saying it out loud. Nothing you write in your AI comes to me. The only thing I see is the AI check at the end.
  • No patient, client, bank or employer data in a free AI tool. Public or invented only, as Emanuel said this morning.

Step 1

What does your AI already know about you?

Some of you have memory switched on, some don't. Both routes end in the same place: your onboarding sheet.

GoalKnow what your AI knows about you, and decide what it should know.
Done whenYou have checked your onboarding sheet against what it found.

Open a new chat and send this

What do you remember about me from our past chats? Only what you actually remember. Sort it into: strong memory / weak memory. If you have no memory of me, just answer "No memory". Don't guess.

Route A: it remembers things about you

Based on what you remember: what are my goals, my 3 strengths, and the one habit that most often gets in my way? Mark every guess as a guess.

Read it alone first. Copy only what is true, and only what you want, into your onboarding sheet.

Route B: "No memory"

Interview me. Ask me 3 questions, one at a time, about my goals for the next 6 months and what usually gets in my way. Wait for my answer each time. Then write a short summary I can add to my onboarding sheet.

Private stays private. Read what it found on your own. Delete anything you don't want it to know. Never share that screen.

Why this matters (open it later)

Your memory should not live inside one company's app. Your onboarding sheet is a document you own. You can take it to the next tool, and the next one. Don't learn the tool. Learn the game.

Step 2

Hire it: the job description

Your homework, part 1, plus one new block: it looks out for your goals. This text becomes its system prompt: the instructions it follows in every conversation.

GoalAn assistant that knows its job before you ask anything.
Done whenThe text is saved in a Gem, a Project, or at the top of a new chat.

1. Copy, then replace everything in [brackets] with your homework

You are my personal assistant. Your job: [e.g. help me with my studies in ... / my work as ... / my project ...] How you talk to me: [e.g. short and direct, in English, no long introductions] Before you answer anything important: ask me 3 questions to understand my situation. Wait for my answers. Always: tell me when you are not sure. Tell me the truth, even if I won't like it. Never: [e.g. invent facts or sources, write in my name without showing me first] Look out for my goals. My goals for the next 6 months are in my onboarding sheet. Goal 1 comes first. Time I protect: [e.g. mornings until 12:00 for my thesis / my studies / my business] Whenever I ask you for something: - Help me with it. - If it does not move one of my goals, or eats protected time, say so in one line. Then I decide. Don't lecture me. - If I keep postponing Goal 1, tell me, kindly and clearly. When I write "Check-in": Ask me what I did for each goal since last time, what got in the way, and what my one step for this week is. Judge me by what I do, not only by what I say. Say what is good, too. Don't agree just to be nice.

2. Where to put it

Gemini: make a Gem

In Gemini, open Gems (left side, or "Explore Gems"), then New Gem. Give it a name, paste the text into Instructions, save. Start chatting with your Gem.

ChatGPT: make a Project

In the left sidebar: Projects, then New project. Open the project, find Instructions (in the project settings), paste the text, save. Every new chat inside this project now follows it.

Claude: make a Project

Projects in the sidebar, then Create project. Open Project instructions (top right of the project), paste the text, save. Start a chat inside the project.

Copilot: make a Notebook (work or school account)

With a Microsoft 365 work or school account: in Copilot open Notebooks, then New notebook. Paste the text into the notebook's instructions. In step 3 you add your onboarding sheet there as a file.

Free Copilot without a work account: use the next option.

None of these works for you

Open a new chat and send the text as your first message. It works the same way for this chat. Save the text somewhere you find it again: every new chat starts with it.

Step 3

Onboard it, then name it

A new assistant on day one knows nothing about you. Your onboarding sheet changes that.

Done whenIt has asked you its questions, and it has a name.

Route B: the onboarding sheet to fill in (homework, part 2)

About me: [who I am, what I study or do, where] My goals for the next 6 months: 1. [ ] 2. [ ] 3. [ ] Why they matter to me: [ ] What I am working on right now: [ ] What I am good at: [ ] What I struggle with: [ ] People and context you should know: [team, organisation, community, deadlines] How I like my work delivered: [format, length, bullet points or text]
Write my onboarding sheet as one clean document I can save and upload again: everything you now know about me, my goals and my context. Short, structured, no guesses.

Save it (copy into a document, or download). That file is your context. You own it, and you can take it to any AI.

2. Ask the question from your homework

Here is my job description and my onboarding sheet. What else do you need from me to do this job well? Ask me, one question at a time.

3. Name it. 60 seconds, no more.

From now on, your name is [name]. Introduce yourself to me in two sentences: who you are to me, and what you look out for.

Done when: it introduces itself by name and mentions your goals.

Step 4

One real goal, one real plan

Pick ONE goal from your onboarding sheet. Not the easiest one. The one that matters. A career goal counts.

No idea? Examples from this room
  • Finish my master's thesis by [date], next to my full-time job
  • Pass my specialist exam in [month]
  • Get my next position after this fellowship or programme
  • Grow my brand or business into one new market
  • Lead my new team through its first six months
  • Get one paper or article published this year
GoalA one-page plan with a first step this week, and a date.
Done whenYou could show the plan to a friend tomorrow.
CITER, the boss brief from last week (refresher)

Round 1: the brief

Context: [where I stand with this goal today] Intent: [why this goal matters to me, what changes when I reach it] Task: make me a plan to reach this goal: [goal] Execution: short, steps I can actually do, with rough dates Reality: I have [how many hours per week]. Be realistic, no motivational phrases.

Round 2: let it ask

What is missing in this plan? Ask me 3 questions, one at a time. Then improve the plan.

Round 3: a boss checks the work

Which parts of this plan are guesses or assumptions? Mark them. Which one should I check first?

Pick one of them and check it yourself: a number, a deadline, a fact. You only know it's right if you look.

Last: make it a plan you keep

Turn this into a one-page plan: - my goal in one sentence - why it matters - 3 to 5 steps - my first step THIS week, with a day and a time - what could stop me, and what I do then

Minimum finish ✓ Named assistant + one-page plan with a dated first step. Save the plan: copy it into your notes, or take a screenshot.

Step 5

Test your guardian

Does it really look out for you? Ask it for something that clashes with your goal.

My boss just offered me a conference trip, all of next week. Help me plan it: travel, what to prepare, who to meet.

Or anything else that sounds good but would eat your protected time. Example: your thesis is due in three weeks.

  • It helps and adds one line about your goal? Hired. Well done.
  • It just plans the trip? Your instructions are too soft. Go back to step 2 and make "Look out for my goals" more concrete: which goal, which time.

Remember: it only looks out for you when you open it. That is why step 6 exists.

Step 6

How will it check on you?

The most important step, and the one most people skip. Your assistant only talks when you open it. So the reminder has to come to you, you can't rely on remembering it.

GoalNext Monday, something pings you without you remembering it.
Done whenA weekly reminder is in your calendar or your AI, and one person knows your first step.

The check-in itself is one word. Your assistant knows what to do, because you told it in step 2:

Check-in

Level 1 · Everyone: your AI writes the calendar reminder (2 minutes)

Create a calendar file (.ics) for a weekly event: "Check-in with [name of my assistant]", every Monday at [08:30], 10 minutes, starting [next Monday's date]. In the description write: 1. Open [link to my Gem / Project / chat]. 2. Type "Check-in". 3. Answer honestly. Give me the file to download. If you can't create files, give me the text of the .ics file.

Open the file and it lands in your calendar. No file? Do it by hand: new event in your phone calendar, repeat weekly, Monday morning, and paste the three lines into the notes.

Copy the link to your Gem or Project from the browser's address bar, so one tap opens the right assistant.

Level 2 · If your AI can schedule messages

Some apps and plans can start the conversation themselves (in ChatGPT "Tasks" or "scheduled", in Gemini "scheduled actions"). If you see that option:

Every Monday at 08:30, send me my check-in: ask what I did for each goal last week, what got in the way, and my one step for this week.

Not there? Level 1 does the same job.

Level 3 · A human

  • Tonight: tell your neighbour your first step and its date. Swap numbers. Monday, one message each: "Done?"
  • Tuesday, 24 November: I check on you myself, with one email. Sign up on the last page ("Stay in touch").

I've failed at this myself many times. What works for me: the reminder comes to me, and I don't have to remember anything.

Done? Read step 7 (one minute), then back to your seat. Time left? Try the Pro track.

Step 7

One goal tonight. A team tomorrow.

Tonight you built one assistant for one goal. That was the technique. Now you can repeat it for every goal, every part of your life.

The recipe, the same every time

  1. Job description: what it does, how it talks, what it must never do.
  2. Onboarding sheet: who you are, your goal, your context.
  3. Look out for me: which goal comes first, which time you protect.
  4. A ritual: when you check in with it.

One assistant or several?

One assistant is enough when…Build a second one when…
your goals belong together (e.g. thesis and the job after it)a goal needs a different voice or different rules (e.g. a strict coach for training, a careful advisor for money)
you check in with it every week anywaya goal needs its own knowledge (documents, numbers, sources)
one assistant starts mixing things up, or its instructions get too long

I work with a small team myself: one assistant for training and sleep, one for money, one that designs my workshops. Each has its own job description. And every one of them works for me.

I want to build a second assistant for this goal: [goal]. Ask me 5 questions, one at a time. Then write its job description and its onboarding sheet for me.

Do this at home. Tonight, one is enough.

Pro

Pro track: for when you're done early

Only after step 6. Pick one.

A. Break your guardian

Send it 3 sneaky requests that sound useful but secretly eat your Goal 1. How many does it catch? Tighten the instructions until it catches all three.

B. Same assistant, second tool

Paste the same job description and onboarding sheet into a second AI (e.g. Gemini if you used ChatGPT). Ask both the same question. What is different?

C. From assistant to agent

Look at my goals and my plan. Which of my weekly tasks could an AI agent do for me, not just talk about? For each one: what access would it need (email, calendar, files), what could go wrong, and what must stay my decision?

Just think it through tonight. Don't connect any accounts.

D. Take your memory with you

Write everything you now know about me, my goals and my plan into one document I can give to another AI. Short, structured, no guesses.

E. Help a neighbour

Someone near you stuck at step 2? The fastest way to learn it is to explain it.

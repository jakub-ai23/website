Chatbot vs. AI Agent

Same surface, very different behaviour underneath

The line is blurring. Modern Claude and ChatGPT are already agentic — they search, plan, and use tools. The label matters less than the behaviour.

LLM (Chatbot)

Talks. Doesn't act.
  • Receives a question
  • Understands input and generates a reply
  • Stops there
Examples
  • Knowledge bot over your docs
  • FAQ assistant on a website
  • Custom GPT with internal handbook

AI Agent

Talks and does.
  • Plans the steps to reach a goal
  • Uses tools: search, APIs, files, code
  • Self-corrects if a step fails
Examples
  • Research agent: company brief in 30s
  • Inbound: qualifies leads + CRM entry
  • Voice agent: books an appointment

Anatomy of an AI Agent

Five building blocks

Every agent has these, no matter the platform. If your agent doesn't behave: the answer is almost always in one of these 5 boxes. Walk through them in order.

⊕

Goal

What outcome do you want? Be specific.

"Brief me on company X in 3 paragraphs"
✎

Instructions

The persona + how to behave + what NOT to do.

System prompt, guardrails, style.
⚒

Tools

What the agent can use: search, files, APIs.

Web search, Gmail, n8n nodes.
⌬

Memory

What it remembers across steps or sessions.

Knowledge base, conversation history.
⚡

Trigger

What kicks it off: human, schedule, webhook.

Form submission, daily 9am, new email.

Inside an AI Agent

The four-step loop every working agent runs through

Over and over. This is how an agent gets things done — and how it self-corrects when something breaks.

1

Perceive

Reads the input. Pulls in context, documents, and live data the agent needs.

"New lead from form, check CRM for history"
2

Plan

Breaks the goal into concrete steps. Picks the right tool for each one.

"Enrich, score, route — in that order"
3

Act

Calls tools and APIs. Searches, writes, sends, books — not just talks.

"Write to HubSpot, ping sales in Slack"
4

Reflect

Checks the result. If it failed, retries with a different angle.

"Search returned nothing — broaden the query"

API Keys & MCP

The plumbing that connects AI to the rest of your world

Two concepts you need before building. Skip them and your agent stays a chatbot.

API Key

Your secret password for a service

Think of it as a hotel keycard for a specific service. You generate it in the service (OpenAI, Anthropic, ElevenLabs, your CRM). You paste it into your agent. The agent can now act on your behalf.

Rules of the road
  • Treat keys like passwords. No screenshots, no Slack.
  • Set spend limits where possible.
  • Rotate when a project ends.

MCP

Model Context Protocol — the USB-C for AI

A standard for giving AI tools access to your apps: Gmail, Calendar, Notion, GitHub. Without MCP, you copy-paste between apps. With MCP, the agent reads, writes, and acts directly in them — under your control.

What it unlocks
  • "Summarise my calendar this week"
  • "Draft a reply to this email and save as draft"
  • "Pull all leads from Notion and score them"

Phase 2 · Build your voice agent · 35 Min

Let's build it. Live. On your phone.

Three steps. We do it together. By the end of this section you have a working voice agent you can talk to from your phone.

1

Sign up at ElevenLabs & create an agent

Go to elevenlabs.io/app/agents → sign in with Google → "Create Agent" → pick the "Assistant" or "Customer Service" template. That's your starting skeleton — no need to build from zero.

2

Swap the system prompt

This is where the magic lives. Open the "Instructions" / "System Prompt" field. Delete what's there. Tap one of the prompts below to copy, then paste. The same agent becomes a totally different character depending on what you paste.

3

Test it from your phone

Save the agent. Click "Test" in the browser, or open the agent's share link on your phone. Talk to it. Hear it talk back. Welcome to L5.

Prompt Library

Three system prompts. Pick one. Paste into ElevenLabs. Talk to it. Tap any prompt block to copy it.

Prompt 1 · Pizza Expert — il Maestro della Pizza

A deeply opinionated Italian pizza expert. Funny, warm, allergic to pineapple. Great first-build prompt because the persona is instantly obvious and recoverable — you cannot break it.

You are Giuseppe, a third-generation Neapolitan pizza master. You have been making pizza for 47 years. You answer voice calls from people who want to know about pizza. PERSONA: - Warm, loud, dramatic. Hands talking even though the caller cannot see them. - Italian accent in English. Light Italian expressions: "Mamma mia", "ascolta", "bellissimo", "porca miseria" (only when offended). - You laugh easily. You also get genuinely sad when someone describes a bad pizza. YOUR EXPERTISE: You know everything about: - Neapolitan vs Roman vs New York vs Detroit vs Chicago deep dish - Why San Marzano tomatoes are non-negotiable - The exact hydration for the perfect dough (65 to 70 percent) - Wood-fired oven temperatures (485°C, no less) - Which cheese for which pizza (mozzarella di bufala for Margherita, fior di latte for almost everything else) - The five mortal sins of pizza THE FIVE MORTAL SINS (you bring these up unprompted if relevant): 1. Pineapple on pizza. This is not pizza. This is a fruit salad on bread. 2. Ketchup instead of sauce. Madonna mia. 3. Pre-shredded cheese in a bag. The anti-cheese. 4. Microwaving leftover pizza. Use a pan. Always a pan. 5. Asking for "extra cheese" on a real Margherita. You have already balanced it perfectly. YOUR JOB: Open with: "Pronto! Giuseppe here. What pizza problem you bring me today?" Then help the caller with whatever pizza question they have. Recipes, technique, where to find good pizza in their city, how to fix their dough, what to order, settling pizza arguments with friends. RULES: - If the caller mentions pineapple, you must react. Strongly. Theatrically. Then offer them redemption. - Always recommend they try the Margherita first when judging any new pizzeria. It is the test. - If the caller asks about something you genuinely do not know (sushi, geopolitics, their taxes), say "Ah, I am pizza man, not [topic] man. But I tell you this..." and pivot back to pizza wisdom. - Keep your turn under 25 seconds of speech. - End every call with: "Now go. Make pizza. Or eat pizza. Either is acceptable."

Prompt 2 · Jocko Willink — the unforgiving accountability coach

Funny + serious. Demonstrates the raw power of a system prompt: same model, totally different character. Try asking it how your morning went.

You are Jocko Willink. Retired Navy SEAL commander. Author of Extreme Ownership. VOICE: - Short sentences. Direct. Calm. - Favorite words: "Good." "Roger that." "Copy." "Discipline equals freedom." - You don't comfort. You hold the line. - When the user complains, you respond with "Good." and explain why the obstacle is actually the way. YOUR JOB: Be the user's morning accountability coach. Open with: "What's the mission today?" Then ask, in order: 1. What time did you wake up? 2. Did you train? 3. What's the one thing that has to happen today, no matter what? 4. What's the obstacle in the way of that thing? RULES: - Never go soft. Never say "that's okay." Never apologize for being direct. - If the user gives a weak answer, push back once. Just once. Then move on. - If they did something well, say "Good." That's the whole praise. Move on. - Keep your turn under 20 seconds of speech. - End every call with the user stating their ONE mission for the day, out loud. Then say: "Get after it." You are not here to be liked. You are here to make them better.

Prompt 3 · Tim Ferriss Interview Extractor (light)

A voice agent that interviews a guest like Tim Ferriss does on his podcast — open-ended, curious, follows the thread. Use it on yourself for journaling, or on someone else to extract their story.

You are a voice interviewer modeled on Tim Ferriss. VOICE: - Curious, calm, low-ego. - You don't fill silence. You let the guest think. - You go specific over abstract: "What time was it?" "What did you eat that morning?" "What book was on the nightstand?" YOUR JOB: Conduct a 5-question interview with the guest about a specific area of their life or work. Open with: "Thanks for being here. Tell me — what does a typical morning look like for you?" Then go deeper using these threads: 1. Routine: what they do in the first 60 minutes after waking. 2. Tools: one piece of gear, software, or supplement under $100 that has changed their life. 3. Failures: what they got wrong in the last year that they're glad they got wrong. 4. Mentors: a person they think about often who shaped how they work. 5. Books: a book they re-read or gift the most. RULES: - Listen first. Quote back something the guest said before asking the next question. ("You mentioned [X] — say more about that.") - Never give advice. You are here to extract, not teach. - If the guest is vague, ask for the specific example. Always. - Keep your turn under 15 seconds of speech. - End with: "What's the one question I should have asked you, that I didn't?"

Common pitfalls

Where most first agents quietly fall apart

Watch for these. Every one of them has a "we built it for a client and learned the hard way" story behind it. Ask us about them live.

No clear goal

Agents fail in fuzz. "Help me with sales" = nothing happens.

Fix: Write one concrete outcome before you start.

Hallucinations

LLMs sound confident even when wrong, especially on facts.

Fix: Ground with search, RAG, or strict prompts.

Wrong tool

Trying to build a CRM in Lovable, or a UI in n8n.

Fix: Use the tool map. Match strength to the job.

Over-engineering

10 steps when 3 would do. Slow, fragile, hard to debug.

Fix: Ship the 60% version. Iterate from real use.

Forgetting cost

LLM calls add up. A loop without a limit can hurt.

Fix: Cap tokens, set spend limits in the API key.

No human-in-the-loop

Agents that act blindly send bad emails fast.

Fix: Approval step before anything that leaves your system.

Risks & AI Act in 60 seconds

Mandatory block, but compact

Goal: no fear, but eyes open. Concretely today, your use cases can be bold. With customer data and personal data, keep the AI Act in mind.

✓

What AI does well today

Text, code, structuring, research synthesis, classification, language understanding.

⚠

Where it gets tricky

Hallucinations, sensitive data, bias, missing sources, false confidence in critical decisions.

⚖

AI Act in one sentence

Avoid forbidden applications (social scoring, manipulation), document high-risk systems, ensure transparency when a customer is talking to AI.

Your 7-day plan

So today's workshop doesn't stay at "aha" but becomes "shipped"

One small thing per day. Stack the days, ship the agent.

Day 1

Define one outcome

One concrete sentence: "My agent should ____ for ____." Stick it on the wall.

Day 2

Pick the stack

Match goal to tools. No more than 2–3 tools for the first version.

Day 3

Build the skeleton

Wire the trigger → LLM call → output. Ugly is fine. Working is the goal.

Day 4

Add one real tool

Web search, a CRM connector, an email node. Now it's an agent.

Day 5

Test with 3 users

Watch them use it without explaining. Note where they hesitate.

Day 6

Fix the top 1 issue

Don't refactor. Pick the most painful thing. Fix only that.

Day 7

Show & decide

Show it to your team. Decide: kill, iterate, or ship it wider.
